# Deep Research: Individualised Glycaemic & BP Targets in Older/Comorbid Patients — and the Office-to-Home Offset

> Researched 2026-08-27 via firecrawl-deep-research (depth: exhaustive — 4 parallel tracks, ~60 sources saved/read).
> Purpose: evidence adjudication for a CDS engine that compares every home reading against a per-patient target set once at enrolment by a junior physician and ratified asynchronously by a remote consultant; until ratified, the engine withholds all recommendations depending on it. Two claims require an evidence base: **(A)** individualised targets outperform uniform ones, and **(B)** home thresholds must sit below office thresholds. The brief demanded the literature's verdict including where it does not support the claims — Track 3's absence findings (senior ratification) are reported plainly as findings.

## Executive Summary

The two claims split cleanly along the evidence. **Claim A — individualised targets outperform uniform ones — is supported in spirit and unproven in form.** No randomized trial has ever assigned patients to individualised-vs-uniform target *systems*; every guideline band in existence is expert extrapolation from trials of *achieved* differences, and the Endocrine Society says so outright ("Although evidence for specific targets is lacking…", [https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/diabetes-in-older-adults](https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/diabetes-in-older-adults)). What the evidence does establish is the two halves that make individualisation rational: uniform tight control caused measured harm (ACCORD all-cause mortality HR 1.22, severe hypoglycemia 16.2% vs 5.1% — [https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa0802743](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa0802743)), and overtreatment of older adults against their own recommended bands is common (~1 million US older adults at HbA1c <7% on insulin/sulfonylurea — [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4426991/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4426991/)) with quantified consequences (97,648 insulin-related hypoglycemia ED visits/year; hypoglycemia hospitalizations now exceed hyperglycemia in older adults — [https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1835360](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1835360)). Target-*driven* care works when bundled with a team and titration rules (Steno-2: mortality HR 0.55 and 7.9 years of lifespan — [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5506099/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5506099/)); no trial isolates the effect of *recording* the target.

**Claim B — home thresholds must sit below office thresholds — is true only relative to the office anchor.** For a 140/90-referenced target, every guideline that publishes a home number uses 135/85 (ISH 2020, NICE, Saudi SHA treatment row — a uniform −5/−5 offset). But ACC/AHA anchors its office target at 130/80 and its own outcome-derived data place the equivalent home threshold at 129–131 mmHg — **offset ≈ 0, not −5** ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6320734/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6320734/); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6592652/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6592652/)). An engine that hard-codes "home = office − 5" would double-offset 130/80-anchored targets. What *is* robust: misclassification without out-of-office measurement — ~30% of clinic-hypertensives are white-coat (treated white-coat risk ≈ treated normotensive, so titrating against office readings adds drug burden without benefit), while ~1 in 3 normal-office patients harbor masked hypertension, whose treated-uncontrolled risk (HR 1.76) approaches sustained uncontrolled ([https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/hypertensionaha.113.02741](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/hypertensionaha.113.02741); [https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1443950619314052](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1443950619314052)).

The ratification gate itself — the junior-sets/consultant-confirms workflow — has **no evidence base anywhere in the target-setting literature**; the closest analogy (radiology over-read of resident reports) shows low junior error rates (major discrepancy 1.7%) and identifies *delay* as the measurable cost of ratification workflows (8.6% of management-changing discrepancies delayed care; 23.2% of discrepancy patients had already been discharged — [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10415655/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10415655/); [https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1546144016302253](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1546144016302253)). And the strongest single warning for the engine design comes from the LIGHT decision-support trial: intensification behavior improved dramatically (adjusted OR 6.87) **but overall BP control did not** — decision support moves intermediate behavior, not outcomes, without workflow and team support ([https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2849453](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2849453)).

## Key Findings

1. **ADA 2026 older-adult bands are explicit and stratified**: healthy A1C <7.0–7.5%; complex/intermediate <8.0% (≥3 comorbidities or ≥2 ADL impairments or mild-moderate cognitive impairment); very complex/poor health "avoid reliance on A1C"; deintensification triggers include severe/recurrent hypoglycemia on insulin/SU *regardless of A1C* ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690186/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690186/)).
2. **Diabetes Canada is the most operational stratification found** — a two-axis table crossing Clinical Frailty Index (1–3 / 4–5 / 6–8 / 9) with therapy hypoglycemia risk: the same patient gets a higher target on insulin/SU (≤7.0% on low-risk therapy vs 7.1–8.0% on high-risk therapy if functionally independent; <8.5% vs 7.1–8.5% if frail/dementia; no A1C target at end of life) ([https://www.diabetes.ca/health-care-providers/clinical-practice-guidelines/chapter-37](https://www.diabetes.ca/health-care-providers/clinical-practice-guidelines/chapter-37)).
3. **Guidelines materially disagree**: ACP 2018 says 7–8% for most and *de-intensify if <6.5%*; AACE targets ≤6.5%; ADA says <7.0–7.5% for healthy older adults — the same patient gets three different numbers from three authorities ([https://www.acponline.org/acp-newsroom/acp-recommends-moderate-blood-sugar-control-targets-for-most-patients-with-type-2-diabetes](https://www.acponline.org/acp-newsroom/acp-recommends-moderate-blood-sugar-control-targets-for-most-patients-with-type-2-diabetes); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6387929/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6387929/)).
4. **The uniform-tight-control retreat rests on three trials**: ACCORD halted early, mortality HR 1.22 (1.01–1.46), MI benefit real (HR 0.76), severe hypoglycemia 16.2% vs 5.1%; ADVANCE mortality-neutral (HR 0.93) with microvascular benefit and hypoglycemia 2.7% vs 1.5%; VADT null primary (HR 0.88), severe hypoglycemia 21.2% vs 9.9%, and at ~10 years no survival benefit (all-cause HR 1.05) ([https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa0802743](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa0802743); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2606812/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2606812/); [https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1414266](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1414266)).
5. **Overtreatment is common and measured**: 61.5% of older US adults sat below 7% — unchanged by health status (62.8% healthy vs 56.4% very complex, P=0.26) — and ~60% of the complex/very-complex group on insulin/SU ≈ 1 million people potentially overtreated ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4426991/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4426991/)); Turkish cohort: 9.8% overtreated by the <6.5% + ≥2-drugs definition ([https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13300-020-00779-0](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13300-020-00779-0)).
6. **Hypoglycemia's measured burden**: 97,648 ED visits/year for insulin-related hypoglycemia and errors; 60.6% with severe neurologic sequelae; in the very elderly (≥80y), 1 in 8 of *all* adverse-drug-event ED visits ([https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1835360](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1835360)); hospitalization rates for hypoglycemia now exceed hyperglycemia (up to 105/100,000 person-years) ([https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2785666](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2785666)).
7. **Deintensification barely happens and its outcome benefit is unproven**: <25% of overtreated patients had treatment reduced even after ACCORD; after a hypoglycemia ED visit, deintensification within 100 days occurred in <50% (24.0% insulin-only); the one verified RCT (Grant 2025, n=450) raised deprescribing (22.8% vs 16.3% at 12 months) but severe hypoglycemia did not significantly differ ([https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circoutcomes.117.003706](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circoutcomes.117.003706); [https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2785666](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2785666); [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40549370/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40549370/)).
8. **The office-to-home mapping is anchor-dependent**: 140/90 office ↔ 135/85 home in ISH 2020, NICE, and Saudi SHA treatment rows; ACC/AHA's 130/80 office maps to home ≈130/80 (outcome-derived 129–131; Ohasama outcome-derived 137/84 for a 140/90 anchor) ([https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.120.15026](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.120.15026); [https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.119.12630](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.119.12630)).
9. **Misclassification without home readings is large and quantified**: white-coat hypertension ~30% of clinic-hypertensives (treated WCH HR 1.16 vs treated normotension — statistically indistinguishable); masked phenomenon in 32% of normal-office patients; masked uncontrolled hypertension in 43% of treated normal-office patients, with risk (HR 1.76) approaching sustained uncontrolled (HR 2.13) ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5338886/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5338886/); [https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/hypertensionaha.113.02741](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/hypertensionaha.113.02741); [https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1443950619314052](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1443950619314052)).
10. **Elderly subgroup trial evidence is positive but population-restricted**: SPRINT ≥75y (NNT 29 primary / 39 death, serious AEs no different), SPRINT frailty post-hoc (no effect modification, ~27% frail), STEP 60–80y China (HR 0.74) — all excluding diabetes, prior stroke, limited life expectancy ([https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1511939](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1511939); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6592652/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6592652/); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC544/](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.123.064003); [https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2111437](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2111437)).
11. **The frail nursing-home counterweight**: PARTAGE — SBP <130 on ≥2 antihypertensives → mortality HR 2.09 (1.16–3.77); but the randomized RETREAT-FRAIL deprescribing trial (mean age 90) found death HR 1.02 (0.86–1.21) — neither harm nor benefit from step-down ([https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2110995](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2110995); [http://www.nephjc.com/news/hypertension-nursinghome](http://www.nephjc.com/news/hypertension-nursinghome)).
12. **BP guidelines disagree in both directions**: ESH 2023 stratifies *upward* with age (140–150 systolic ≥80y) and uniquely makes <120/70 a Class III harm; ESC 2024 treats age as non-modifying up to 85 and defaults to 120–129 in all adults; NICE targets <150/90 ≥80y; Saudi SHA recommends SBP <130 for >65y "if tolerated" — stricter than ESH for the same band ([https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.124.24173](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.124.24173); [https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng136/chapter/Recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng136/chapter/Recommendations); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10069676/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10069676/)).
13. **Inertia is enormous and age-biased**: 80.1% of Japanese patients above HbA1c goal not intensified at the next visit; UK median time-to-intensification 2.9/1.9/1.6 years at thresholds 7.0/7.5/8.0%; therapeutic inertia occurred at 53.8–60.5% of above-goal visits *inside SPRINT* and 89.6% of eligible US outpatient visits, rising with age independent of frailty/cognition/gait (P-interaction >0.10) ([https://www.ovid.com/01555620-202602000-00014](https://www.ovid.com/01555620-202602000-00014); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10438422/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10438422/)).
14. **CDSS moves intensification, not control**: LIGHT (93 practices): intensification at uncontrolled visits 47.3% vs 11.6% (OR 6.87) but no improvement in BP control vs usual care ([https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2849453](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2849453)).
15. **SDM/negotiated targets are modest**: pooled HbA1c −0.14% (−0.26 to −0.02) across 17 RCTs with two-thirds of trials null; no hypoglycemia outcomes reported anywhere ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11290692/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11290692/)).
16. **Senior ratification of junior-set targets: zero direct evidence exists** — searched directly; the adjacent radiology over-read literature shows junior major-discrepancy 1.7% (critical 0.007% per Mellnick) and delay as the ratification cost ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10415655/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10415655/); [https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1546144016302253](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1546144016302253)).
17. **Saudi/Gulf guidelines exist, endorse individualisation in prose, and haven't operationalized it**: SHA 2023 gives concrete subgroup BP bands and says "clinical management should be individualized"; no Saudi MOH diabetes clinical guideline exists (only an ADA-verbatim patient page); a 23-CPG systematic review of older-adult diabetes targets contains *zero* Gulf guidelines; Saudi audits still measure fixed ADA cutoffs (15.7% attainment) ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10069676/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10069676/); [https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/healthawareness/educationalcontent/diseases/diabetic/pages/diabetes-management.aspx](https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/healthawareness/educationalcontent/diseases/diabetic/pages/diabetes-management.aspx); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11581819/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11581819/); [https://www.mdpi.com/1648-9144/61/10/1856](https://www.mdpi.com/1648-9144/61/10/1856)).
18. **Ramadan has operational tools and Saudi validation**: the 14-domain IDF-DAR risk score is in clinical use; Madinah validation (n=466): hypoglycemia by risk tier 4.7%/13.9%/28.3% (p<0.001); break-fast thresholds <70/>300 mg/dL; no guideline changes the numeric BP target during Ramadan — only timing (diuretics at iftar) ([https://www.mdcalc.com/calc/10522/international-diabetes-federation-diabetes-ramadan-alliance-idf-dar-fasting-risk-assessment](https://www.mdcalc.com/calc/10522/international-diabetes-federation-diabetes-ramadan-alliance-idf-dar-fasting-risk-assessment); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10807666/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10807666/); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6218385/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6218385/); [https://www.jhah.com/en/health-wellbeing/ramadan-health-guide/managing-health-conditions-while-fasting/blood-pressure-medication-and-fasting-in-ramadan/](https://www.jhah.com/en/health-wellbeing/ramadan-health-guide/managing-health-conditions-while-fasting/blood-pressure-medication-and-fasting-in-ramadan/)).

## Detailed Analysis

### Track 1 — Individualised HbA1c targets and the harm of over-tight control

**The bands and their stratifiers.** ADA 2026 (Table 13.2): healthy (<7.0–7.5%; a lower individual goal permitted if achieved "without recurrent or severe hypoglycemia or undue treatment burden"); complex/intermediate (<8.0%); very complex/poor health ("avoid reliance on A1C"; manage to avoid hypoglycemia and symptomatic hyperglycemia). The complex band is defined concretely: ≥3 serious comorbidities (arthritis, cancer, HF, depression, emphysema, falls, hypertension, incontinence, stage 3+ CKD, MI, stroke), or ≥2 ADL impairments, or mild-to-moderate cognitive impairment. Frailty appears both as a stratifier (13.7b: <8.0% and/or TIR ≥50%, TBR <1%) and in the plain statement that very complex older adults "receive minimal benefit from stringent glycemic goals" ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690186/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690186/)). The ADA/ACCF/AHA post-ACCORD position statement adds the historical framing: less stringent goals "may be appropriate" for limited life expectancy, advanced complications, extensive comorbidities; and — critically for an engine — ACCORD's mortality excess "was likely related to the overall treatment strategies for intensifying glycemic control … not the achieved A1C per se," so a patient sitting low on metformin alone is not the target of the warning ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2606812/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2606812/)). Diabetes Canada encodes the therapy axis explicitly ([https://www.diabetes.ca/health-care-providers/clinical-practice-guidelines/chapter-37](https://www.diabetes.ca/health-care-providers/clinical-practice-guidelines/chapter-37)). The Endocrine Society tailors targets to health status and hypoglycemia-risk medication while admitting the evidentiary hole ([https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/diabetes-in-older-adults](https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/diabetes-in-older-adults)). ACP goes furthest toward leniency: 7–8% for most, de-intensify below 6.5%, symptoms-only for life expectancy <10 years, and no A1C targets at all for adults ≥80 ([https://www.acponline.org/acp-newsroom/acp-recommends-moderate-blood-sugar-control-targets-for-most-patients-with-type-2-diabetes](https://www.acponline.org/acp-newsroom/acp-recommends-moderate-blood-sugar-control-targets-for-most-patients-with-type-2-diabetes)).

**The trials.** ACCORD: 10,251 high-risk patients, target <6.0%, achieved 6.4% vs 7.5%; stopped at 3.5 years; all-cause mortality 257 vs 203 (HR 1.22, P=0.04), CV death HR 1.35, nonfatal MI reduced (HR 0.76), no primary-outcome benefit (HR 0.90, P=0.16), hypoglycemia requiring assistance and >10 kg weight gain more frequent (P<0.001); no clear mechanism identified ([https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa0802743](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa0802743)). ADVANCE: 11,140 patients, target ≤6.5%, mortality neutral (HR 0.93), microvascular benefit (HR 0.86), macrovascular null (0.94), severe hypoglycemia 2.7% vs 1.5%. VADT: 1,791 veterans (entry A1C 9.4%), primary null (HR 0.88); 10-year follow-up found 8.6 fewer MACE per 1,000 person-years but **no all-cause mortality benefit (HR 1.05, P=0.54)**, with the authors explicitly refusing to endorse an A1C <7.0% performance measure ([https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1414266](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1414266)). Subset benefit across all three trials was confined to shorter diabetes duration, lower entry A1C, no established CVD ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2606812/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2606812/)) — i.e., the individualisation logic has a real empirical skeleton, just never tested as a policy.

**Overtreatment and its harms.** Lipska (NHANES 2001–2010): 61.5% of older US adults with diabetes at HbA1c <7% with *no difference by health status*; ~1 million potentially overtreated on insulin/SU; harms listed: increased mortality, CVD, falls and accidents, dementia, low HRQoL; a VA study found ~50% of veterans with ≥1 major hypoglycemia risk factor intensively treated anyway ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4426991/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4426991/)). Geller: 97,648 ED visits/year; 29.3% hospitalized; ≥80-year-olds: ED and hospitalization rates 2× and 5× those of 45–64; the authors' conclusion sentence is practically the engine's design brief: "individualizing glycemic targets by balancing hypoglycemia risks with long-term benefits… is appropriate" ([https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1835360](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1835360)). Alexopoulos: hypoglycemia hospitalizations now exceed hyperglycemia in older adults ([https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2785666](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2785666)).

**Deintensification.** Thresholds exist (ADA Table 13.3: severe/recurrent hypoglycemia on insulin/SU regardless of A1C; wide excursions; polypharmacy; ACP <6.5% → reduce/stop), but action is rare (<25% overall; 21% even at A1C <6%; no increase after ACCORD), the verified RCT improved process (deprescribing 22.8% vs 16.3%) without a significant severe-hypoglycemia reduction, and no mortality/CV endpoint has ever been tested ([https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circoutcomes.117.003706](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circoutcomes.117.003706); [https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2785666](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2785666); [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40549370/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40549370/); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13355641/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13355641/)). The OPTIMAL deintensification trial could not be retrieved this session — its endpoints remain unverified here.

### Track 2 — BP targets and the office-to-home offset

**The mappings, precisely.** ISH 2020: office ≥140/90 ↔ home ≥135/85 ↔ daytime ABPM ≥135/85 ↔ night ≥120/70 ([https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.120.15026](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.120.15026)). NICE: clinic 140/90 → HBPM 135/85 (<80y) and **145/85 (≥80y)** — the only guideline publishing an age-shifted *home* target ([https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng136/chapter/Recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng136/chapter/Recommendations)). ACC/AHA: office 130/80 → home 130/80 — offset zero, "supported by regression- and outcome-derived home thresholds: Dallas Heart Study home SBP 130 (black), 129 (white), 131 (Hispanic) ≈ office 130" ([https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.119.12630](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.119.12630)). Ohasama's outcome-derived home threshold for a 140/90 office anchor was 137/84, "which supported the… threshold of 135/85" (same source). ESC 2024 mandates Class I out-of-office measurement for diagnosis *and titration* but the retrieved text does not state its numeric home cutoff; ESH 2023 likewise ([https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.124.24173](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.124.24173)). Saudi SHA: treatment consideration at >140/90 office / ≥135/85 home; classification at ≥130/80 — the diagnosis and treatment rows use different anchors and must not be conflated ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10069676/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10069676/)).

**Misclassification without out-of-office measurement.** White-coat: 10–15% of the general population, ~30% of clinic-hypertensives, up to 25–30% of outpatient-hypertension-clinic attenders ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5338886/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5338886/); [https://www.escardio.org/communities/councils/cardiology-practice/scientific-documents-and-publications/ejournal/volume-14/White-coat-hypertension-not-so-innocent/](https://www.escardio.org/communities/councils/cardiology-practice/scientific-documents-and-publications/ejournal/volume-14/White-coat-hypertension-not-so-innocent/)). Masked: 11% overall, **32% of normal-office patients**, MUCH in 13% overall / 43% of treated normal-office patients ([https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1443950619314052](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1443950619314052)). Prognosis: IDHOCO — treated WCH HR 1.16 (0.79–1.72) ≈ treated normotension (i.e., office-driven titration of white-coat patients adds drugs for no prognostic gain); treated masked uncontrolled HR 1.76 (1.23–2.53) ≈ sustained uncontrolled 2.13 ([https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/hypertensionaha.113.02741](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/hypertensionaha.113.02741)). HONEST: morning home SBP ≥145 with office <130 → HR 2.47 ([https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.119.12630](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.119.12630)).

**Age/frailty stratification and its limits.** SPRINT (standardized office measurement): intensive <120 vs <140, HR 0.75 (0.64–0.89); ≥75y subgroup NNT 29 (primary), 39 (death), serious AEs no different; but SPRINT *excluded* diabetes, prior stroke, standing SBP <110, eGFR <20, LVEF <35%, limited life expectancy ([https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1511939](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1511939); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6592652/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6592652/)). Frailty did not modify benefit (n=9,306; 26.7% frail) ([https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.123.064003](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.123.064003)). STEP (60–80y, China): HR 0.74 with hypotension slightly up, dizziness/syncope/fracture/renal not different ([https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2111437](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2111437)). The nursing-home counterweight: PARTAGE — SBP <130 on ≥2 drugs → mortality HR 2.09 ([https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2110995](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2110995)); RETREAT-FRAIL randomized step-down in the same population (mean age 90): death HR 1.02 — the observational signal was nullified in both directions ([http://www.nephjc.com/news/hypertension-nursinghome](http://www.nephjc.com/news/hypertension-nursinghome)). J-curve: observational DBP nadirs scatter 70–85 mmHg; trial post-hocs show no treatment-effect modification at low BP; the review concludes low DBP is "confounding or reverse causation," and the one guideline invoking a hard floor (ESH <120/70, Class III) is explicitly challenged in the peer-reviewed guideline comparison ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8685549/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8685549/); [https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.124.24173](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.124.24173)).

**Guideline disagreements, explicitly.** ESH 2023: <130/80 <65y, <140/80 65–79y, 140–150 ≥80y, initiation ≥160 if ≥80y, Class III below 120/70. ESC 2024: 120–129 all adults if tolerated; DBP 70–79 (IIb); rejects age-stratified targets because age is not an effect modifier "at least up to 85 years." ACC/AHA 2017→2025: <130/80 for all adults. NICE: <140/90 / <150/90 ≥80y clinic; 135/85 / 145/85 home. Saudi SHA: SBP <130 >65y if tolerated; CAD 120–130 (130–140 if elderly); DBP floors 70 (CAD), 60 (LVH) ([https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.124.24173](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.124.24173); [https://targetbp.org/best-practices/guidelines/](https://targetbp.org/best-practices/guidelines/); [https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng136/chapter/Recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng136/chapter/Recommendations); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10069676/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10069676/)).

### Track 3 — Does an explicitly recorded target change anything?

**Target-driven care works when bundled; documentation alone is untested.** Steno-2 (n=160, 21.2 years): all-cause mortality HR 0.55 (0.36–0.83), median lifespan gain 7.9 years, CVD-event-free time +8.1 years — but it bundles explicit targets with intensive multifactorial team care; n=160; single Danish center ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5506099/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5506099/)). Hypertension standardized-treatment-protocol meta (16 RCTs, 59,945): SBP −6.7 mmHg (3.7–9.8) vs usual care; the headline control-rate comparison (57.0% vs 23.9%) is internally inconsistent with its own results section (RR 1.27, CI crossing 1, I²=99%) — treat cautiously; the SBP effect is the robust finding ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11773677/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11773677/)). Generic "goal setting" as a behavioral intervention is weak: of 35 meta-analytic models across 25 outcomes, none strong, weak for 8 (HbA1c among them); elderly-collaborative-goal-setting review: "compelling evidence for its benefits is lacking" ([https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362341669_](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362341669_Concordance_of_Adherence_Measurement_Using_Self-Reported_Adherence_Questionnaires_and_Medication_Monitoring_Devices_An_Updated_Review); [https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/10775587221113228](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/10775587221113228); [https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12877-017-0534-0](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12877-017-0534-0)).

**Inertia: the strongest-supported claim in this file.** Japan (J-DREAMS, n=5,683): 80.1% not intensified at the next consultation despite being above goal — and ~half of apparent inertia was physicians watching improving-but-still-high trends, i.e., ambiguity about *which target applies* manufactures both true and false inertia; the authors conclude "establishing individualized HbA1c targets at each consultation… [is] essential" ([https://www.ovid.com/01555620-202602000-00014](https://www.ovid.com/01555620-202602000-00014)). UK median time-to-intensification: 2.9/1.9/1.6 years at HbA1c thresholds 7.0/7.5/8.0%; Spain 17.1 months ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9498838/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9498838/)). SPRINT secondary analysis: TI at 53.8–60.5% of above-goal visits *even inside a protocolized trial*; NAMCS: 89.6% of eligible visits; TI rose with age independent of frailty, cognition, gait speed — implicit age heuristics, and the authors explicitly recommend "a defined threshold for intervention" as the counter ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10438422/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10438422/)). Monte-Carlo modeling: removing inertia at initiation raises 10-year BP control from 15.9% to 89.0% under low measurement error ([https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.120.15866](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.120.15866)).

**The decision-support caution.** LIGHT (93 Chinese practices, n=4,612): CDSS raised intensification at uncontrolled visits from 11.6% to 47.3% (adjusted OR 6.87, 4.90–9.64) and each 0.22-point intensification-score rise tracked −3.8 mmHg SBP — yet overall BP control did not improve vs usual care. Decision support that only nudges intensification does not automatically change outcomes without the surrounding workflow ([https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2849453](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2849453)).

**SDM and negotiated targets.** Pooled HbA1c −0.14% (−0.26 to −0.02) over 17 RCTs; two-thirds of individual trials null; publication bias detected; the two trials most specifically about personalized targets (OPTIMAL n=141; PANDAs n=167) were null ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11290692/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11290692/)). A 2026 Taiwanese decision-aid study improved knowledge/satisfaction/decisional intention but states clinical outcomes "require further longitudinal evaluation" ([https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12911-026-03426-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12911-026-03426-z)). No SDM trial reported hypoglycemia outcomes.

**Senior ratification: the absence finding.** Direct searches for consultant sign-off of junior-set chronic-disease targets returned nothing — no outcome, delay, safety, or abandonment studies exist in this domain. The nearest analog is radiology over-read: major discrepancy 1.7%, minor 8.3% across 9,072 studies; 2.0% of reports management-changing, of which 88.1% no patient impact, 8.6% care delay, 1.3% recalled patient, 1 death (0.7% of the management-changing subset); Mellnick (153,420 exams): 1.4% overall discrepancy, critical 0.007%; 23.2% of discrepancy patients already discharged ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10415655/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10415655/); [https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1546144016302253](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1546144016302253)). Radiology quantifies junior error rates and the *cost of waiting* — it does not show that requiring over-read improves outcomes, and perceptual-error transfer to judgment domains (where the "correct" target is negotiated) is an analogy, not evidence.

### Track 4 — Saudi/Gulf applicability and home care

**Guidelines exist; individualisation is prose, not practice.** Saudi SHA/NHC 2023 hypertension guideline: "Clinical management should be individualized, and careful consideration should be given to special conditions and patient groups," with concrete subgroup bands — >65y: SBP <130 if tolerated; CAD: 120–130 (130–140 if elderly), DBP <80 but >70; HF: <130 preferably not below 120; LVH DBP floor 60; frail/elderly: monotherapy acceptable ≥80y, postural BP monitoring mandated, loop diuretics/alpha-blockers to avoid unless indicated ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10069676/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10069676/)). For diabetes: no Saudi MOH clinical guideline was found — only a patient-education page copying ADA verbatim (<7%, preprandial 80–130, postprandial <180) with a single individualisation sentence ("Blood glucose goals may vary according to age, any additional health problems…") and no subgroup bands ([https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/healthawareness/educationalcontent/diseases/diabetic/pages/diabetes-management.aspx](https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/healthawareness/educationalcontent/diseases/diabetic/pages/diabetes-management.aspx)). The Emirates Diabetes Society consensus states targets are "individualised and discussed with the patient and caregiver" and "in line with international guidelines," but its numeric tables are images and could not be extracted ([https://karger.com/dde/article/26/1/1/93644/Emirates-Diabetes-Society-Consensus-Guidelines-for](https://karger.com/dde/article/26/1/1/93644/Emirates-Diabetes-Society-Consensus-Guidelines-for)). The 23-CPG systematic review of older/frail-adult diabetes targets — the reference corpus for individualisation — contains zero Gulf guidelines by text search ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11581819/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11581819/)). Meanwhile Saudi quality audits measure pure fixed ADA cutoffs and report 15.7% HbA1c-goal attainment, while citing that "knowing the HbA1c level and understanding the individual glycemic target is connected with improved glycemic control" ([https://www.mdpi.com/1648-9144/61/10/1856](https://www.mdpi.com/1648-9144/61/10/1856)). Gulf T1D registry: mean HbA1c 8.11%, only 27.4% <7% ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8805329/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8805329/)). The individualisation endorsement-vs-measurement mismatch is documented, not speculative.

**Home-care target-setting: nobody publishes it.** MOH's official Home Health Care Services page lists services and visit logistics (referral 72h pre-discharge, first visit one week post-discharge) and names hypertensives and diabetics as eligible groups — with no mention of who sets glycaemic/BP targets, what they are, or any review cadence; a linked Arabic-only "Home Healthcare Guide" PDF exists but could not be retrieved (timeout) — its contents unverified, not proven absent ([https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/awarenessplateform/patientsrights/pages/homehealthcare.aspx](https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/awarenessplateform/patientsrights/pages/homehealthcare.aspx)). International nurse-led/home programmes demonstrate BP benefit but are silent on target authorship — they titrate *toward* preset guideline targets; a junior-proposes/consultant-ratifies per-patient process "is not something I found anywhere in the home-care literature" ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9843665/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9843665/); [https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2024.1378144/full](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2024.1378144/full)). The citable cadence anchor is ADA: assess glycemic status at least quarterly; reassess targets against individualized criteria; review hypoglycemia occurrence and risk at every encounter ([https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/45/Supplement_1/S83/138927/6-Glycemic-Targets-Standards-of-Medical-Care-in](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/45/Supplement_1/S83/138927/6-Glycemic-Targets-Standards-of-Medical-Care-in)). ADA's architecture is also explicitly time-varying: "a goal that might be appropriate for an individual early in the course of their diabetes may change over time" (same URL).

**Ramadan.** The IDF-DAR 2021 framework is operational and locally validated: 14-domain risk score (hypoglycemia frequency, regimen, HbA1c, eGFR, frailty/cognition, labor, fasting hours), tiered as low/moderate/high risk with "Allow flexibility in glycemic targets during Ramadan" as explicit management guidance ([https://www.mdcalc.com/calc/10522/international-diabetes-federation-diabetes-ramadan-alliance-idf-dar-fasting-risk-assessment](https://www.mdcalc.com/calc/10522/international-diabetes-federation-diabetes-ramadan-alliance-idf-dar-fasting-risk-assessment); [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35016991/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35016991/)). Madinah validation (n=466, Ramadan 2022): 56.9% high risk; hypoglycemia 4.7%/13.9%/28.3% by tier (p<0.001); 53.2% of high-risk patients fasted completely; T1DM broke fasts mostly "in the first 5 hours… attributed to failure to reduce the dose of the rapid- or short-acting insulin before the Suhoor meal"; reviewers: the tool "might overestimate the risk of fasting for some patients" ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10807666/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10807666/)). Hard break-fast thresholds: glucose <70 or >300 mg/dL ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6218385/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6218385/)). Saudi CGM data add a twist: during fasting, *hyperglycemia* dominates (48% vs 10% time-in-range inversion; TAR rising in 2022), attributed partly to "overprotective behavior" by patients/physicians — and the same diurnal pattern appeared in 49 non-diabetic fasters ([https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2025.1399990/full](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2025.1399990/full); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6218385/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6218385/)). For hypertension: BP is stable during fasting (Tunisian ABPM n=60: 129/74 → 128/73, NS; season-transfer caveat) and guidance concerns timing (diuretics at iftar; shift to once/twice-daily schedules; HBPM "strongly encouraged… does not break your fast," ideally before suhoor and after iftar) — **no guideline changes the numeric BP target during Ramadan** ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8796675/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8796675/); [https://www.jhah.com/en/health-wellbeing/ramadan-health-guide/managing-health-conditions-while-fasting/blood-pressure-medication-and-fasting-in-ramadan/](https://www.jhah.com/en/health-wellbeing/ramadan-health-guide/managing-health-conditions-while-fasting/blood-pressure-medication-and-fasting-in-ramadan/)).

## The Verdict on the Two Claims

**Claim A — "Individualised targets outperform uniform ones."**
**SUPPORTED IN SPIRIT; UNPROVEN IN FORM.** No trial has ever randomized individualised-vs-uniform target *systems*; the Endocrine Society admits "evidence for specific targets is lacking." What the evidence establishes: (1) uniform tight control caused measured harm in the population where individualisation matters most (ACCORD mortality HR 1.22; severe hypoglycemia 16.2% vs 5.1%); (2) overtreatment against recommended bands is common (~1M US older adults; 9.8% in Turkey by a stricter definition) and carries quantified harm (97,648 insulin-hypoglycemia ED visits/year; hypo hospitalizations exceeding hyperglycemia; dementia/falls links); (3) all four major guideline families encode stratification — and disagree with each other's numbers, which means an engine must declare a guideline lineage, not claim neutrality; (4) target-driven care changes hard outcomes when bundled with team/titration support (Steno-2 HR 0.55; STP meta −6.7 mmHg) while documentation alone and patient goal-setting per se are weakly supported; (5) implicit-target inertia is enormous and age-biased (TI at 89.6% of eligible visits; age OR 1.32 independent of frailty), and its own authors prescribe "a defined threshold" — the mechanism an explicit per-patient target implements. The engine is *consistent with* the evidence; it cannot claim band-level validation, and it should say so in-product.

**Claim B — "Home thresholds must sit below office thresholds."**
**TRUE ONLY RELATIVE TO THE ANCHOR.** For 140/90-anchored targets, the home threshold is uniformly 135/85 (ISH 2020, NICE, Saudi SHA treatment row; outcome-derived Ohasama 137/84). For 130/80-anchored targets, ACC/AHA's own outcome-derived data put home ≈ office (129–131) — offset ≈ 0. A hard-coded "minus 5" rule is therefore wrong for half the guideline lineage space. What is robust without qualification: the misclassification evidence (white-coat ~30% of clinic-hypertensives with treated-WCH risk indistinguishable from normotension; masked phenomenon in 32% of normal-office patients with treated-MUCH risk ≈ sustained uncontrolled) and the Class I mandate for out-of-office measurement in titration (ESC 2024). NICE additionally age-shifts the home target (145/85 ≥80y). No RCT defines the optimal home *treatment* target — the AHA review states RCTs are needed; the engine's offset must be implemented as an anchor-relative mapping, not a universal constant.

**Embedded claim — "Senior ratification of a junior-set target is a safeguard."**
**NO EVIDENCE EXISTS — REPORTED AS A FINDING, PER BRIEF.** Direct searches returned nothing in the target-setting domain; the radiology over-read analogy shows low junior major-discrepancy rates (1.7%; critical 0.007%) and delay as the measurable cost (8.6% of management-changing discrepancies delayed care; ~23% of discrepancy patients already discharged when caught). Ratification is a safety hypothesis grounded in analogy and first principles; it must be instrumented (override rate, ratification latency vs time-to-intensification, abandonment rate) — and the async consultant queue must be designed so it does not become the clinical inertia the explicit target exists to prevent.

## Contrarian Views And Risks

- **"Your bands are committee opinions."** Every guideline number is expert extrapolation; ACP, ADA, AACE, AGS, and Diabetes Canada assign different targets to the same patient. A target-setting engine inherits whichever lineage it chooses — neutrality does not exist ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6387929/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6387929/)).
- **"The achieved-A1C paradox can corrupt your overtreatment flag."** ACCORD's excess mortality tracked the intensification *strategy*, not low A1C itself; a flag keyed only to the number will fire on well patients on metformin alone and miss dangerous regimens. The flag must encode medication class (insulin/SU) exactly as Diabetes Canada's two-axis table does ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2606812/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2606812/); [https://www.diabetes.ca/health-care-providers/clinical-practice-guidelines/chapter-37](https://www.diabetes.ca/health-care-providers/clinical-practice-guidelines/chapter-37)).
- **"Asymptomatic hypoglycemia undermines the loose-band rationale."** Diabetes Canada warns that CGM studies show prolonged asymptomatic hypoglycemia even at higher A1C — the premise underpinning relaxed bands in frail patients "will need to be re-examined" ([https://www.diabetes.ca/health-care-providers/clinical-practice-guidelines/chapter-37](https://www.diabetes.ca/health-care-providers/clinical-practice-guidelines/chapter-37)).
- **"Deintensification's benefits are unproven."** The best RCT raised deprescribing rates without significantly reducing severe hypoglycemia; no hard-outcome trial exists; a deintensification recommendation from the engine is hypothesis, not proven therapy ([https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40549370/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40549370/); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13355641/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13355641/)).
- **"The frail-elderly BP story is contradictory."** PARTAGE (SBP <130 on 2+ drugs → mortality HR 2.09) vs RETREAT-FRAIL (randomized step-down: HR 1.02, death 61.7% vs 60.2% in a population where both arms' event rates far exceeded design assumptions). Neither aggressive control nor deprescribing is validated in the frailtest patients; ESH's 140–150 and ESC's 120–129 for the same age band cannot both be right ([https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2110995](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2110995); [http://www.nephjc.com/news/hypertension-nursinghome](http://www.nephjc.com/news/hypertension-nursinghome); [https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.124.24173](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.124.24173)).
- **"CDSS ≠ outcomes" (the LIGHT warning).** Intensification OR 6.87, zero control improvement. The engine's ratification gate and recommendation engine must be evaluated on attainment, not on whether clinicians click accept ([https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2849453](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2849453)).
- **"The Gulf mismatch is an opening and an indictment."** Regional guidelines say "individualise" while regional audits measure fixed cutoffs at 15.7% attainment; that mismatch is documented and citable — but it also means there is no local validation of any stratified band, and the engine would be introducing the concept, not importing proven local practice ([https://www.mdpi.com/1648-9144/61/10/1856](https://www.mdpi.com/1648-9144/61/10/1856); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11581819/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11581819/)).
- **"Ramadan flexibility is asymmetric in practice."** The IDF-DAR framework relaxes targets on paper, but Saudi CGM data show patients/physicians over-protect on the *upper* side (hyperglycemia dominating during fasting); an engine applying symmetric flexibility would fight observed behavior ([https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2025.1399990/full](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2025.1399990/full)).

## Open Questions

- ACCORDION (ACCORD long-term mortality follow-up) and pooled ACCORD+ADVANCE+VADT meta-analysis HRs were not retrieved this session — needed if the engine ever quotes pooled numbers.
- OPTIMAL deintensification trial endpoints (Zaslavsky 2020) could not be retrieved; both attempted scrapes returned wrong papers.
- Does any home-care or virtual-ward program anywhere publish its target-setting authorship workflow? (None found in four tracks; the Home Healthcare Guide PDF remains unread.)
- The Emirates consensus numeric target tables are images — extracting them requires the original document, not the web version.
- What ratification latency does asynchronous consultant review actually produce in practice? No analog data beyond radiology next-day finals; the engine's own rollout will generate the first numbers.
- Is NICE's age-shifted home target (145/85 ≥80y) outcome-validated or purely conventional? No retrieved source tests it.
- Ramadan BP target adjustment: no guideline changes the number — is that evidence of stability or absence of study? Only one Tunisian ABPM study, season-transfer-limited.
