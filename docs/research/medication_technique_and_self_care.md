# Deep Research: Medication Technique & Self-Care Execution Failure in Home-Based Diabetes/Hypertension Care

> Researched 2026-08-27 via firecrawl-deep-research (depth: exhaustive — 4 parallel tracks, ~90 sources consulted, ~80 citable).
> Purpose: evidence adjudication for a CDS engine behavior. The engine's central claim: a class of clinically significant finding exists only inside the patient's home, appears in no health record, and corrupts the signals a dose-titration algorithm depends on — so the engine withholds the titration recommendation the corrupted reading would otherwise justify and issues the correction instead.
> The brief demanded the literature's verdict **including where it does not support the claim**. Track-level "What the evidence does NOT support" findings are integrated throughout and adjudicated in the dedicated Verdict section.

## Executive Summary

The evidence base is real, quantified, and — with important exceptions the engine must respect — supportive of the thesis. **The home-measured failure class exists and is large.** On physical examination, lipohypertrophy affects **38%** of insulin injectors pooled across 26 studies, **39.7% in a Saudi cohort and 47.7% in Saudi adolescents**, with 51–95% of affected patients continuing to inject into the lesions ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5934253/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5934253/); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7336011/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7336011/); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11058417/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11058417/)). Sensor-logger data show **79% of home insulin storage logs deviated out of range** and **17% recorded sub-zero freezing** ([https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332590842](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332590842)). Observed demonstrations — not questionnaires — reveal the damage: in Saudi Arabia, **67% of patients believe their injection technique is correct** while direct observation found 45% with insufficient dwell time, 46% failing to prime, and 77% refrigerating in-use pens ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12080473/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12080473/)); across 54,354 observed inhaler tests spanning four decades, only **31% of technique was correct** ([https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012369216475719](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012369216475719)).

**The corruption magnitudes are measured, not hypothetical.** Injecting insulin into lipohypertrophied tissue cuts insulin exposure (Cmax −25%, AUC −22%) and is associated with unexplained hypoglycemia (pOR 6.98) and glycemic variability (pOR 5.24) ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11058417/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11058417/)). Poorly resuspended NPH cartridges contained **5–214% of nominal insulin** ([https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10560676/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10560676/)). Fruit residue on unwashed fingers produced false readings up to **+15 mmol/L (270+ mg/dL)** — enough to trigger an insulin dose an algorithm would endorse ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3041187/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3041187/); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5423336/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5423336/)). A too-small BP cuff shifted extra-large-arm patients' mean from 125/79 to **144/87 mmHg** ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10407761/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10407761/)).

**The record-void claim holds with one wording discipline.** No study we found documents routine structured capture of technique, storage, or home-observation findings in EMRs; a 1.8-million-patient validation study found only **13% of free-text-extractable clinical concepts had any structured counterpart** ([https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e66910/](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e66910/)), and in Bahrain, injection sites "were never checked in all except one (1.1%) patient" ([https://www.bahrainmedicalbulletin.com/SEPT_2017/SEPT2017_LIPO.pdf](https://www.bahrainmedicalbulletin.com/SEPT_2017/SEPT2017_LIPO.pdf)). Self-report systematically overestimates execution — median **+17%** adherence vs electronic monitoring ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4917812/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4917812/)) — so the engine cannot trust patient-reported technique.

**The engine's corrective behavior has interventional support — and three hard constraints.** Fixing technique cut severe hypoglycemia from 15.8%→4.1% and permitted ~20% insulin-dose reductions in controlled trials ([https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4540/6/3/22](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4540/6/3/22); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7947164/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7947164/)) — but benefits fade without **≥6-monthly reinforcement** ([https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4540/6/3/22](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4540/6/3/22)), HbA1c may stay flat (that is expected, not failure), and four tempting triggers are evidence-null: heat-induced potency loss at ordinary home excursions (Cochrane finds no clinically relevant loss up to 37°C for months — [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10627263/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10627263/)), cuff-over-clothing error, needle reuse→HbA1c harm, and expired-strip error magnitude. The claim survives — narrower, sharper, and more defensible than it entered.

## Key Findings

1. **Lipohypertrophy is the anchor lesion**: 38% pooled prevalence (1.9–73.4% range), 41.8% in a 37-study meta, 86.5% when ultrasound is used; Jordan 37.3%, Saudi T2DM 39.7%, Saudi T1D adolescents 47.7%, Bahrain (ultrasound) 36.8% ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5934253/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5934253/); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11058417/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11058417/); [https://brieflands.com/journals/ijem/articles/17694](https://brieflands.com/journals/ijem/articles/17694); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7336011/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7336011/); [https://www.bahrainmedicalbulletin.com/SEPT_2017/SEPT2017_LIPO.pdf](https://www.bahrainmedicalbulletin.com/SEPT_2017/SEPT2017_LIPO.pdf)).
2. **LH corrupts exactly the signals titration uses**: injecting into LH → insulin aspart Cmax −25%, AUC0–4h −22%, higher variability; associated with unexplained hypoglycemia pOR 6.98 (3.30–14.77), glycemic variability pOR 5.24, HbA1c +0.55%, TDD +7.7 IU — all cross-sectional ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11058417/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11058417/)).
3. **Cause chain quantified**: incorrect rotation pOR 8.85 and needle reuse pOR 3.20 predict LH ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11924065/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11924065/)); in a Chinese observed cohort of 1,370, 94% reused needles, one-third rotated sites, 48% had LH ([https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jep.14226](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jep.14226)).
4. **Home storage reality**: 78.8% of personal sensor logs deviated out of range; refrigerated insulin out of range 11.3% of time; 17% of sensors recorded freezing; carried insulin only 0.5% out of range ([https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332590842](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332590842)). Only 45% of households globally (62% in high-income group incl. Saudi) store medicines properly ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12821371/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12821371/)). But Cochrane found **no clinically relevant potency loss** at 25–37°C for 2–4 months — freezing, not heat, is the better-evidenced home-storage harm ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10627263/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10627263/)).
5. **NPH resuspension failure is the single most quantified technique error**: cartridge content 5–214% of nominal; 65% deviated >20%; only 9% mixed >10 times; post-education hypoglycemia 1.0→0.4 episodes/month, HbA1c unchanged (8.9→8.4, p=0.07) ([https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10560676/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10560676/)). Prevalence-relevant: 32.8% of Saudi and 58.3% of Jordanian cohorts use premixed insulin ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7336011/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7336011/)).
6. **Glucose readings corrupted by hands**: unwashed fingers after peeling fruit: grape +14.6 mmol/L (93→360 mg/dL, some >600 "HI"); pawpaw +14.5 mmol/L; single alcohol swab insufficient; tap-water washing fully corrects ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3041187/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3041187/); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5423336/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5423336/)).
7. **Strip environment errors**: 15 min at 42°C/83% RH → ~+33 mg/dL; 50-day field: 18°C rise → +37 mg/dL; heat-stressed strips read falsely high, cold-stressed falsely low; one patient with pouch-stored strips, falsely low readings, abandoned medication, true BG 21.8 mmol/L ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5032951/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5032951/)). No quantified study of expiry per se.
8. **BP cuff mismatch is the largest measured BP error**: correct-cuff-needed-large patients +4.8 mmHg, extra-large +19.5 mmHg (mean 125/79→144/87); ~40% of US adults need a large cuff but consumer devices ship one regular cuff ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10407761/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10407761/)). Talking +4–19 mmHg; unsupported arm +4.9; insufficient rest +4.2–11.6; **cuff over clothing: no significant effect in systematic review** ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5278896/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5278896/)).
9. **Self-report cannot substitute for observation**: self-report overestimates adherence vs MEMS by median 17% (range −21 to +75), ~90% of formal agreement tests reject agreement ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4917812/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4917812/)); observed inhaler technique 31% correct over 40 years with zero improvement; Saudi insulin cohort: 67% self-assess technique as correct vs observed pervasive error ([https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012369216475719](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012369216475719); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12080473/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12080473/)).
10. **Correcting technique works — on the right endpoints**: severe hypoglycemia 15.8→4.1%, unexplained hypos 47→16%, variability 64→30% (LIMO); TDD −15% vs controls (Tianjin RCT); insulin requirement −20.7% (ISTERP-1); HbA1c effects inconsistent and often null — **and single-session training is "virtually worthless" without ≥6-monthly refreshers** ([https://www.endocrine-abstracts.org/ea/0079/ea0079009](https://www.endocrine-abstracts.org/ea/0079/ea0079009); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7947164/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7947164/); [https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4540/6/3/22](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4540/6/3/22)).
11. **Caregivers execute and err**: 57.8% of informal caregivers committed ≥1 medication error/year (13.5 errors/caregiver-year); injected-medication dosing errors 19–44%; error odds triple with multiple caregivers ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11574791/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11574791/)). Saudi caregiver-execution evidence is pediatric (T1D incidence 109.5/100,000); caregiver KAP is self-reported (93% "always monitoring") and therefore ceilings, not measurements ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13096650/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13096650/)).
12. **Foot self-care gap in Saudi Arabia**: only 41.5% inspect feet daily, 40.8% walk barefoot at home, 77.7% never had a specialist foot exam ([https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_183_21](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_183_21)); among hospitalized DFU patients in Jeddah, **84.9% underwent amputation** ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8689050/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8689050/)).
13. **Retention decays fast**: 40–80% of medical information is forgotten immediately; almost half of what is remembered is wrong ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC539473/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC539473/)); ISTERP-2's no-refresher arm "progressively abandoned best practices" and ended worse than baseline ([https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4540/6/3/22](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4540/6/3/22)).
14. **The record void is documented indirectly**: only 13% of free-text-extractable concepts have structured counterparts; brown-bag participation <1/3; 68% of transitions carry ≥1 unintentional medication discrepancy ([https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e66910/](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e66910/); [https://impartalliance.msu.edu/publication/medication-reconciliation-in-an-outpatient-geriatrics-clinic-does-accuracy-improve-if-patients-brown-bag-their-medications-for-appointments/](https://impartalliance.msu.edu/publication/medication-reconciliation-in-an-outpatient-geriatrics-clinic-does-accuracy-improve-if-patients-brown-bag-their-medications-for-appointments/); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11654400/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11654400/)).

## Detailed Analysis

### Track 1 — Insulin handling and injection technique

**Storage.** The only sensor-based study (n=338, 400 loggers attached to patients' own insulin) found deviations out of recommended range in 78.8% of logs; refrigerated insulin was out of range 11.3% of the time (mean 2h43m/day), carried insulin only 0.5%; **17% of sensors recorded sub-zero exposure** — fridges freeze insulin ([https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332590842](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332590842)). Manufacturer data and the 2023 Cochrane review show unopened vials stable up to 25°C for ~6 months and 37°C for ~2 months, opened vials to 12 weeks at 37°C, oscillating 25–37°C without clinically relevant loss ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10627263/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10627263/)). The worst controlled finding — 14–18% potency loss after 28 days at constant 32–37°C ([https://www.researchgate.net/publication/26864186](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/26864186)) — showed no significant in-vivo effect in rabbits. Phoenix hot-climate survey: 37% "protect" medications by leaving them home; HbA1c highest in hottest quarter but explicitly correlational ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2956813/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2956813/)). **No Gulf/MENA sensor-based storage study exists**; the closest are Indian and Sri Lankan practice surveys (Colombo: 56% cold-chain failure during transport; 1.5% storing backup in a deep freezer) retrieved within the Vimalavathini citation stream.

**Lipohypertrophy.** Prevalence and consequences above (Key Findings 1–2). Patients keep injecting into LH because it hurts less; up to 95.3% continue into lesions ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11058417/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11058417/)). Saudi practice (n=202): only 60.3% change needle every injection, 10% single-site, 13.9% "haphazard" rotation; 91.1% had HbA1c >7% ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7336011/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7336011/)). Bahrain: 36.8% ultrasound-confirmed LH with sites never checked in 98.9% of patients ([https://www.bahrainmedicalbulletin.com/SEPT_2017/SEPT2017_LIPO.pdf](https://www.bahrainmedicalbulletin.com/SEPT_2017/SEPT2017_LIPO.pdf)).

**Other quantified errors.** Premature needle withdrawal under-delivers 1.6–20.2% of the selected dose depending on pen and dose; only 39% of Canadian respondents hold ≥10 s ([https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/dia.2015.0067](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/dia.2015.0067)). NPH resuspension failure: content 5–214% of nominal; a mechanical mixer needs ≥20 cycles ([https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10560676/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10560676/)). Needle length: 38% of patients showed fluid leakage/intradermal deposition with sitting-posture skin thickness averaging 3.3 mm; Jordan still uses 8 mm needles in 89.9% ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10233969/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10233969/); [https://brieflands.com/journals/ijem/articles/17694](https://brieflands.com/journals/ijem/articles/17694)); IM absorption is "only slightly faster at rest but variably and substantially increased with exercise" — a hypoglycemia mechanism ([https://diabetesjournals.org/clinical/article/37/3/227/32887/](https://diabetesjournals.org/clinical/article/37/3/227/32887/)). **Needle reuse**: 40–96% prevalence across cohorts, but the dedicated 25-study meta-analysis found **no association with worse glycemic control and no infection signal** — reuse harms via pain and (at ≥5 uses) LH risk, not acutely via HbA1c ([https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002074891630030X](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002074891630030X)).

**Interventional evidence.** Tianjin RCT (n=210): protocolized technique correction + 10–20% dose reduction → TDD −15% vs controls; between-group HbA1c 0.31% per-protocol only ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7947164/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7947164/)). LIMO (n=146): injecting-into-LH 51%→8%; severe hypoglycemia 15.8%→4.1%; HbA1c unchanged at 7.5% ([https://www.endocrine-abstracts.org/ea/0079/ea0079009](https://www.endocrine-abstracts.org/ea/0079/ea0079009)). ISTERP-1 (RCT, n=318): reinforced education → intra-lesion injection 59.9%→1.9%, severe hypoglycemia 16.4%→0.6%, insulin requirement −20.7% ([https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4540/6/3/22](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4540/6/3/22)). Practical rule with direct CDS relevance: when moving injections out of LH, cut dose ~20% immediately — absorption improves abruptly and hypos follow if the old dose persists ([https://diabetesjournals.org/clinical/article/37/3/227/32887/](https://diabetesjournals.org/clinical/article/37/3/227/32887/)).

### Track 2 — Oral medication timing and measurement technique

**Sulfonylurea + skipped meal.** Mechanism and guideline acknowledgment are solid: "incorrect timing related to meal" plus fasting is a named hypoglycemia cause; SU hypoglycemia risk persists 24–36 h post-dose; ADA explicitly instructs checking "whether the person… is skipping meals or inadvertently repeating doses" ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8696639/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8696639/); [https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/48/Supplement_1/S266/157556/13-Older-Adults-Standards-of-Care-in-Diabetes-2025](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/48/Supplement_1/S266/157556/13-Older-Adults-Standards-of-Care-in-Diabetes-2025)). The ingredients quantified separately: timing adherence falls 54.2 percentage points from once-daily to four-times-daily regimens ([https://www.jmcp.org/doi/pdf/10.18553/jmcp.2012.18.7.527](https://www.jmcp.org/doi/pdf/10.18553/jmcp.2012.18.7.527)); 19.5% of community-dwelling adults ≥70 skip ≥1 traditional meal (diabetes aOR 1.26) ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10035663/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10035663/)). **No study quantifies the combined dose-then-skip incidence** — the engine may claim documented clinical concern, not documented prevalence.

**Metformin without food.** 71-RCT meta: diarrhea RR 2.45, nausea RR 1.64, abdominal pain RR 1.49; ~5% discontinue from GI severity; most discontinuations occur in the first third of therapy; no significant discontinuation difference vs comparators ([https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2022.975912/full](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2022.975912/full)). "Silent dose reduction" (under-dosing without reporting) is inferred, not measured.

**Glucose meter/strip errors.** Fruit residue: Hirose — grape 93→360 mg/dL (some >600 "HI"), orange 98→171, kiwi 89.5→183; one alcohol swab insufficient (grape still 274); 5 wipes still elevated (131); tap water normalizes ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3041187/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3041187/)). Olamoyegun (n=50): pawpaw 354.9±222 mg/dL, apple 273.5±138.2; error persisted ≥1 h; authors warn of "inadvertent admission of excess insulin resulting in severe hypoglycemia" ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5423336/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5423336/)). Environmental: 42°C/83% RH for 15 min → +~33 mg/dL; 50-day field: 18°C rise → +37 mg/dL in one meter; heat-stressed strips false-high, cold-stressed false-low; loose-pouch strips case: falsely low readings → medication abandoned → true BG 21.8 mmol/L ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5032951/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5032951/)).

**Home BP technique errors.** Cuff(SZ) randomized crossover (n=195): using a regular cuff when a large is needed +4.8 mmHg SBP, extra-large +19.5 mmHg (125/79→144/87); NHANES: regular cuff fits only 51% of adults; 5 mmHg error could misclassify ~84M people globally ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10407761/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10407761/); ACC summary: 39% misclassified hypertensive / 22% of hypertensives misclassified normotensive — [https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2023/06/05/19/18/why-is-cuff-size-so-important-and-other-factors-that-affect-accurate-bp-measurement](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2023/06/05/19/18/why-is-cuff-size-so-important-and-other-factors-that-affect-accurate-bp-measurement)). Talking +4–19 mmHg (6/6 studies); unsupported arm +4.87; insufficient rest +4.2–11.6; single reading +3.3–10.4; full bladder +15 ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5278896/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5278896/)). Device validation: AHA statement — "Many HBPM devices on the market have not been validated," no home recalibration standards exist, wrist monitors poorly reliable for self-measurement, smartphone BP apps poor and mostly unvalidated ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11409525/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11409525/)). **Cuff-over-clothing: systematic review found no significant effect** (4 studies; meta +0.6 mmHg ns) — treat the "up to 50 mmHg" folk figure as unsupported for thin sleeves ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5278896/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5278896/)).

### Track 3 — Self-report versus observation (the load-bearing question)

**The discrepancy is real and large.** Saudi southern-region cross-section (n≈?): **67% answered "Yes, my technique is correct"** while observed demonstrations showed 45% dwell <5 s, 46% no priming or unaware of it, 37% no skin fold, 43% stopper not cleaned, 77% refrigerating in-use pens; observed failures tracked with hypoglycemia episodes (p=0.015–0.056) ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12080473/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12080473/)). The cleanest two-measure comparison is inhalers: observed technique >82% inadequate while questionnaires agreed with observation mainly when patients were performing correctly — self-report overestimates correctness ([https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9777884/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9777884/)). Forty years of observed inhaler tests (144 articles, 54,354 subjects): 31% correct, no improvement across decades ([https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012369216475719](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012369216475719)).

**Adherence questionnaires vs objective measures.** 117 studies, 251 within-sample MEMS comparisons: self-report overestimates by median 17% (range −21 to +75); pill count +8%; ~90% of formal agreement tests reject agreement ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4917812/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4917812/)). Glaucoma patients: 31% overestimated doses taken, 74% overestimated timing ([https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319634897_Concordance_of_Adherence_Measurement_Using_Self-Reported_Adherence_Questionnaires_and_Medication_Monitoring_Devices_An_Updated_Review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319634897_Concordance_of_Adherence_Measurement_Using_Self-Reported_Adherence_Questionnaires_and_Medication_Monitoring_Devices_An_Updated_Review)). Self-report remains the dominant instrument in 72% of studies ([https://www.mdpi.com/2039-4403/15/10/370](https://www.mdpi.com/2039-4403/15/10/370)).

**What homes actually contain.** Household storage meta-analysis (61 studies, 27 countries, n=34,297; includes 4 Saudi household studies): proper storage 45% overall, 62% in the high-income group including Saudi Arabia ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12821371/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12821371/)). Brown-bag reviews are recommended practice, yet **fewer than one-third of geriatric subjects brought their medications when asked** ([https://impartalliance.msu.edu/publication/medication-reconciliation-in-an-outpatient-geriatrics-clinic-does-accuracy-improve-if-patients-brown-bag-their-medications-for-appointments/](https://impartalliance.msu.edu/publication/medication-reconciliation-in-an-outpatient-geriatrics-clinic-does-accuracy-improve-if-patients-brown-bag-their-medications-for-appointments/)). Even inside hospitals, 68% of critically ill older adults carried ≥1 unintentional medication discrepancy at transitions ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11654400/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11654400/)). Honest boundary: a classic pharmacist-home-visit yield study (prescribed-vs-found medicine discrepancies in homes) was **not retrieved** in this session — that specific prevalence claim remains unsourced.

**Record capture (the falsification test).** No study found documenting routine structured EMR capture of technique/storage/observation findings. The documentation literature points the other way: in 1.8M Dutch primary-care patients, only 13% of free-text-extractable concepts had structured counterparts; notes are heavily copy-paste/autopopulated; critical context is pushed into free text invisible to rules engines ([https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e66910/](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e66910/); [https://psnet.ahrq.gov/web-mm/copy-and-paste-notes-and-autopopulated-text-electronic-health-record](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/web-mm/copy-and-paste-notes-and-autopopulated-text-electronic-health-record); [https://patientsafetyj.com/article/118587-free-text-as-part-of-electronic-health-record-orders-context-or-concern](https://patientsafetyj.com/article/118587-free-text-as-part-of-electronic-health-record-orders-context-or-concern)). Where LH findings exist, they live in research datasets and narratives, made by inspection/palpation/ultrasound — and sites are simply never checked in routine care (Bahrain: 98.9% never checked). Care moved remote makes it worse: inhaler-technique support occurred in only one-third of telephone appointments vs two-thirds face-to-face ([https://www.kcl.ac.uk/making-asthma-inhaler-checks-more-effective](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/making-asthma-inhaler-checks-more-effective)). Wording discipline: "not captured as structured data in any study we found," not "proven absent everywhere."

### Track 4 — Caregiver execution and the diabetic foot

**Caregivers execute — and err.** Spanish home-care study (n≈685 informal caregivers): **57.8% committed ≥1 medication error in the past year** (9,236 errors total, mean 13.5/caregiver/year); dosing errors with injected medication ran 19–44% among subgroups administering injections; **error odds triple when multiple caregivers are involved** — directly relevant to Saudi multi-caregiver households ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11574791/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11574791/)). The 16-study scoping review describes caregivers assuming "substantial responsibility… often without adequate preparation or support" ([https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/opn.70094](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/opn.70094)). Saudi evidence is pediatric-T1D-shaped: caregiver KAP (n=397) self-reports 93% "always/often monitoring" pump use — a ceiling figure given the self-report literature ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13096650/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13096650/)); MOH CSII pilot qualitative: "we had to help him take insulin and measure sugar" ([https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/29986702261435179](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/29986702261435179)); caregiver burden: 57.3% moderate, 28.4% high ([https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-7143233/latest](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-7143233/latest) — preprint). **Observed-demonstration error data exist for patients, not caregivers** — a genuine evidence gap the engine should declare.

**Training works, but decays.** ISTERP-1: reinforced education → intra-lesion injection 1.9% vs 59.9%, HbA1c 8.2→6.2 (intervention only), insulin requirement −20.7%; ISTERP-3: single-session-only improvements "slight and transient" — "the virtual worthlessness of a single training session"; ISTERP-2: withdrawing refreshers → progressive abandonment ending **worse than baseline** ([https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4540/6/3/22](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4540/6/3/22)). Saudi pediatric CSII pre-post (n=30): HbA1c 8.82→7.18 at 6 months, no control group ([https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12887-025-06018-4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12887-025-06018-4)). Kessels: 40–80% of medical information forgotten immediately, ~half of what is remembered is wrong ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC539473/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC539473/)); teach-back review: knowledge improves, **retention evidence limited and contradictory** ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6590951/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6590951/)). Elderly-caregiver-specific retention: no source found.

**Foot self-care in Saudi Arabia.** Qassim (n=260): 56.5% "good knowledge" yet 41.5% daily inspection, 40.8% barefoot at home, 39.2% ever advised by a physician, 77.7% never had a specialist exam ([https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_183_21](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_183_21)); Alsaleh (n=357): 79.3% good knowledge but 30.8% good practice ([https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1319016421000633](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1319016421000633)). Stakes: Jeddah hospitalized DFU cohort (n=358): 84.9% amputation rate, 7-year mortality 20% ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8689050/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8689050/)); diabetic foot disease accounts for ~80% of non-traumatic lower-limb amputations ([https://johs.com.sa/article/JOHS.2022.2402](https://johs.com.sa/article/JOHS.2022.2402)). Hot-surface burns: case-report level only (hot water bottles; "about three-quarters of thermal injuries result from the victim's own actions") ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1463094/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1463094/)); **Gulf hot-pavement burn epidemiology: no source found**.

## The Verdict on the Central Claim

The brief demanded adjudication, not advocacy. The claim has four components; the evidence grades them differently.

**Component 1 — "A class of clinically significant finding exists only inside the patient's home."**
**SUPPORTED (strong).** Every track independently quantified a home-only failure class with direct clinical consequence: LH 38–48% (Gulf cohorts 36.8–47.7%), out-of-range storage in 79% of sensor logs, ≥1 technique error in 100% of Canadian survey respondents (mean 3.5 errors), 57.8% of caregivers committing medication errors, proper household medicine storage only 45–62%, unwashed-hand false glucose elevation up to +15 mmol/L, cuff mismatch +4.8 to +19.5 mmHg, NPH content 5–214% of nominal. None of these are exotic; all are common, measurable at home, and each has a documented link to hypoglycemia, variability, misclassification, or dose inflation.

**Component 2 — "…appears in no health record."**
**SUPPORTED (with wording discipline).** No study found documents routine structured capture of technique, storage, or home-observation findings; the documentation literature shows observations live (if anywhere) in free text with only 13% of free-text concepts reaching structured fields; in routine Gulf practice, injection sites were never checked in 98.9% of insulin patients, and <1/3 of geriatric patients even bring medications for brown-bag review. The engine should say "not captured as structured data in any study we found" — not claim a proven universal absence.

**Component 3 — "…and corrupts the very signals a dose-titration algorithm depends on."**
**SUPPORTED AT MECHANISM LEVEL; END-TO-END CHAIN UNMEASURED.** The PK and measurement evidence is real: LH → Cmax −25%/AUC −22% with increased variability (the exact readings a titration engine consumes); NPH resuspension → 5–214% content; unwashed hands → +5–15 mmol/L; cuff mismatch → ±5–20 mmHg — each individually large enough to flip a titration decision. LH is associated with unexplained hypoglycemia (pOR 6.98) and variability (pOR 5.24) — the two phenomena an algorithm reads as "needs more insulin" or "unstable patient." **But no study anywhere has measured the full causal chain home-execution → corrupted reading → wrong titration decision → harm.** The engine must therefore present technique-failure detection as a *risk flag triggering inspection*, not as a quantified potency discount or a certain corruption event. The associations are cross-sectional; reverse causality (high doses driving LH) is acknowledged in the primary literature.

**Component 4 — The engine's behavior: "withhold the titration the corrupted reading would justify; issue the correction instead."**
**SUPPORTED, CONDITIONAL ON THREE DESIGN CONSTRAINTS.**
(a) *Correction is evidence-based and dose-changing*: interventional studies consistently permit/require immediate 10–20% insulin-dose reductions when technique is fixed, with hypoglycemia and variability improvements (severe hypos 15.8→4.1%; insulin requirement −20.7%). The "withhold-and-correct" behavior is a digital version of a documented clinical maneuver.
(b) *Reinforcement is not optional*: single-session training is "virtually worthless"; withdrawal of refreshers produces worse-than-baseline technique. Any engine issuing corrections must schedule ≥6-monthly reinforcement or it manufactures false confidence.
(c) *HbA1c is the wrong success metric*: technique correction reliably improves hypoglycemia/variability/dose burden while HbA1c may stay flat or even rise (because dose was cut). If the engine's evaluation framework expects HbA1c wins from technique corrections, it will misjudge itself as failing.

## Contrarian Views And Risks

- **"You're building an engine on cross-sectional associations."** The LH-outcome evidence is almost entirely cross-sectional without follow-up; the meta-analysis authors themselves state causality cannot be concluded, and several individual studies found no LH–hypoglycemia association. The engine's detection logic reads signals that also have mundane explanations ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11058417/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11058417/)).
- **"Insulin is tougher than your thesis assumes."** The Cochrane thermal-stability review and manufacturers' own data undercut the hot-climate-potency narrative: no clinically relevant loss at 25–37°C for months. A heat-centric engine trigger would flag patients whose insulin is fine — alert fatigue by another route ([https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10627263/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10627263/)).
- **"Correction can backfire."** The Moscow RCT saw TDD *rise* after training (clinician titration up in a low-LH population); ISTERP-2's unrefreshed arm ended worse than baseline; contaminated Tianjin controls improved just from seeing their lesions. Technique intervention without a maintenance schedule can produce regression that looks like engine failure ([https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13300-017-0315-y](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13300-017-0315-y); [https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4540/6/3/22](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4540/6/3/22); [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7947164/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7947164/)).
- **"Record-void is an inference, not a measurement."** No retrieved study proves structured capture is absent system-wide; the claim rests on absence of evidence plus indirect documentation literature. A regulator or partner could produce a counterexample (e.g., a home-care platform capturing device handling as structured data).
- **"HbA1c nulls will be read as failure."** LIMO and Jehle showed zero significant HbA1c change despite dramatic hypoglycemia/variability improvements — stakeholders judging the engine on HbA1c will misjudge it ([https://www.endocrine-abstracts.org/ea/0079/ea0079009](https://www.endocrine-abstracts.org/ea/0079/ea0079009); [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10560676/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10560676/)).
- **"Self-report criticism boomerangs."** The strongest technique-error prevalences in this file (Canada 100% ≥1 error) are themselves self-reported surveys; observed data (Xi'an 94% reuse, 48% LH) exist for patients but not caregivers. The engine's own detection claims must not rest on the same self-report instruments it distrusts ([https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13300-020-00913-y](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13300-020-00913-y)).
- **"The Gulf end-to-end chain is unmeasured."** No sensor-based Gulf storage study, no Gulf caregiver-observation study, no home-vs-clinic measurement divergence study. Every Saudi-specific number in this file is a single-center cross-section (Jeddah n=202, Riyadh n=174, Qassim n=260, Bahrain n=95, southern region). The prototype's assumptions are plausible link-by-link but unproven as a chain in the target population.
